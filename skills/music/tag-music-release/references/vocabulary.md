# Tag Vocabulary

The rating scale and the words a tag line may use, grouped by category. The user owns this list. Add a word only with the user's approval. Keep set phases in set order and every other category alphabetical. Each word names one distinct feel; when two words mean the same feel, keep one.

## Rating

Rekordbox stars, exactly one per track. This scale is fixed; it takes no custom words.

- 0 stars: unrated
- 1 star: interesting, rarely played
- 2 stars
- 3 stars: good, regular track
- 4 stars
- 5 stars: special, a track you love

## Set phase

Where the track sits in a house or techno set.

- Start — establish the mood and groove; welcoming, not too intense.
- Build — gradually add momentum: stronger drums, bass, and more recognizable hooks.
- Peak — the highest-energy section; biggest, most driving tracks.
- Sustain — keep the floor energized without continually escalating; vary textures while holding intensity.
- Release — ease the tension or provide emotional payoff; can reset the room before the next round/phase.

## Mood

- Aggressive — forceful, intense, hard-hitting energy.
- Dark — shadowy, ominous, moody, or heavy.
- Emotional / Introspective — reflective, vulnerable, personal, or heartfelt.
- Floating / Dreamy — hazy, atmospheric, weightless, and immersive.
- Happy — bright, warm, playful, and uplifting.

## Vibes

- Acid — squelchy, resonant synth lines; raw and trippy.
- Afro — African-influenced percussion, polyrhythms, and groove.
- Arabic — Middle Eastern-inspired scales, melodies, or percussion.
- Arpeggiated — repeating notes that climb or cycle in a pattern.
- Asian — broad Asian musical references; better used with a specific tradition in mind.
- Beats — rhythm-forward and drum-focused.
- Bouncy — springy bass and playful rhythmic lift.
- Broken — off-kilter or broken-beat rhythms rather than a straight four-on-the-floor pulse.
- Classic — timeless, traditional dance-music language.
- Dirty — rough-edged, raunchy, distorted, or gritty.
- Disco — uplifting, funky, four-to-the-floor dance feel.
- Dope — confident, stylish, and cool; a broad positive flavor tag.
- Dreamy — soft, floating, hazy, and atmospheric.
- Driving — persistent forward momentum, usually for a dance floor.
- Dubby — echo, reverb, spacious bass, and stripped-back repetition.
- Electro — electronic, often robotic or futuristic rhythm and synth character.
- Epic — large-scale, dramatic, emotional, and climactic.
- Fun — lighthearted, immediate, playful energy.
- Funky — syncopated rhythm, bass groove, and soulful movement.
- Glitchy — stutters, digital artifacts, chops, and deliberate sonic errors.
- Goofy — quirky, silly, or cartoonish.
- Grindy — tough, rough, repetitive, and abrasive.
- Head nodding — mid-tempo, weighty groove made more for feeling than jumping.
- Jazzy — richer chords, improvisational feel, and jazz-influenced harmony.
- Joyful — bright, warm, optimistic, and celebratory.
- Latin — Latin-influenced percussion and rhythmic swing.
- Loopy — hypnotic repetition built around repeating phrases.
- Melodic — memorable tunes and chord movement take priority.
- Poppy — catchy, polished, hook-led, and accessible.
- Punchy — sharp drums, strong transients, and immediate impact.
- Sosig — likely a playful label for thick, “sausage-like” compressed bass/synth sound; not a standard genre term.
- Soulful / Musical — expressive vocals, warm chords, and strong harmony; ideal for vocal house.
- Spiritual — gospel-like, meditative, uplifting, or transcendent.
- Swung — rhythms that lean or shuffle rather than being mechanically straight.
- Techy — streamlined, synthetic, precise, and club-functional.
- Tropical — sunny, breezy, percussion-led, often with Caribbean or beach-like colors.
- Weird — unconventional sounds, odd arrangements, or unexpected choices.

## Instruments

The most prominent instruments, bass type, and vocal type.

- Acapella — isolated voice, with little or no instrumental backing; useful for intros, breakdowns, or dramatic transitions.
- BoomingBass — large, deep, weighty low end that makes the track feel physically powerful.
- Chanting — repeated, rhythmic vocal phrases; communal and hypnotic.
- Congas — hand-drum percussion with warm, rolling Latin/Afro-house movement.
- GrindyBass — gritty, distorted, or growling bass; tougher and more underground.
- Guitar — plucked or rhythmic melodic parts; can bring funk, disco, soul, or warmth.
- Hi Hats — crisp cymbal patterns that create pace, swing, and forward motion.
- Horns — brass such as trumpet or sax; bright, celebratory, soulful accents.
- Organ — sustained keyboard chords, often warm and gospel-tinged; classic house color.
- Pads — soft, sustained synth layers that create atmosphere and space.
- Piano — chord stabs, riffs, or melodies; especially associated with piano house.
- Rap — rhythmically delivered lyrics; adds attitude, narrative, or hip-hop influence.
- Singing — melodic vocals; can provide emotion, hooks, and a song-like focus.
- Spoken Word — speech rather than singing or rapping; intimate, poetic, or message-driven.
- Strings — violin/cello-like parts, sampled or synthesized; lush, emotional, and dramatic.
- Sweeps — rising or falling effects used to build anticipation or mark transitions.
- WalkingBass — a moving bass line that travels through notes rhythmically; bouncy, jazzy, or disco-influenced.
